How to play

  • Deployment: before each battle, pick who fights and where they stand. Units left in reserve take no part. The opposition is listed on the left.
  • Charge Time (CT): every unit gains CT equal to its Speed each tick. At 100 CT it takes a turn. The Turn Order panel forecasts who acts next.
  • Turn: Move, then Act (or Act, then Move), then choose a facing. Skipping Move or Act gives +20 CT toward your next turn. A move can be taken back until the unit acts, unless something happened on the way.
  • Facing: attacks from the side halve the target's evasion; attacks from behind cannot be evaded. Face your enemies when you Wait.
  • Friend or foe: your side stands on smooth blue rings and wears blue; the enemy stands on jagged red rings and wears red. The bar over every head is framed in the same colour, so a figure hidden behind a wall still tells you whose it is. The wedge on the ring points the way that unit is facing.
  • Height: attacking from 2+ tiles above deals 10% more damage; from below, 10% less. Jump limits how high you can climb.
  • Elements: fire, ice, thunder, earth, holy, dark and water. Creatures and equipment answer them: a bomb drinks fire and burns in ice, a dire wolf fears fire, a fell knight feeds on dark and dreads holy, and the drowned drink water and burn in thunder. The targeting preview tells you before you commit.
  • The north: hollow knights, wights and the Court's weavers absorb ice and fear fire and holy light. Snow is walked like grass; river ice is walked like stone. Stop and Slow are the Court's favourite tricks: a Warden's Iron Footing or a Warmth Stone shrugs them off.
  • Guns and machines: a gun reaches four tiles or more and does not care about height, so the Concord's gunners will shoot down at you from anywhere. Their sentinels and hounds are brass: lightning gets through it, fire does not.
  • Charged abilities: spells and Aim shots resolve after their charge fills. Targets that move out of the area are missed. Friendly fire is real.
  • Falling: a downed unit counts down three turns before it is carried from the field. Revive it in time and it stays. Losing your leader loses the battle.
  • Crystals: where a unit is carried off, a crystal remains. End a move on it and that unit is restored in full, HP and MP. The enemy will go for one when hurt; so should you.
  • Saves: three slots. New Game takes an empty one, or asks which to write over; Continue takes the latest; Load Game lists them all. A lost battle offers Try again from the results, without the story before it, and any flagged stop on the map can be fought again for half the pay.
  • Camp tabs: the camp is four pages: Road (the night before), Company (tavern and errands), Cities, and Options. The unit page in Formation is three: Unit, Gear and Skills.
  • Cities: towns on the map are held by someone until you fight them open. An open city stays open: its tavern hires recruits already trained in an advanced trade, and its market sells arms the wagon never carries. Tap a city on the map, or use the Cities card at camp.
  • Errands: at camp, send a unit who is not the leader away on an errand. They miss the next battle or two and come back with gil, JP in the job they left in, and sometimes something found.
  • Objective: shown in the bar above. Some battles end when the enemy commander falls, or when you have held out long enough.
  • Jobs: earn JP by acting. Spend it in Formation on abilities and passives. Reach job levels to unlock advanced jobs, and equip a second skillset from any job you have studied.
  • Passives: one reaction, one support and one movement ability at a time. Learn them in one job and wear them in any.
  • Equipment: buy gear at the shop and fit it in Formation, or press Optimize. Weapons set your power and reach; armour, shields and accessories do the rest.
  • Baggage: everything spare, shelved by category and kind: swords with swords, robes with robes. Hand a piece to anyone who can wear it from the shelf, sell it there, or take a piece off someone in the table below.
  • Know your enemy: point at or tap any enemy and its card lists the skills it can use.
  • Threat: tap an enemy while choosing an action, or during deployment, and the tiles it could strike on its next turn turn violet. Tap it again to clear.
  • Auto: the Auto button, or A, hands your turns to the computer until you press it again. It fights like the enemy does: fine for a training battle, no substitute for you in a hard one.
  • Speed: the 1× button, or F, runs battles at double or triple time. Nothing is skipped; it all just happens faster. Handy once you have watched enough enemy turns.
  • Turning the field: the board turns a quarter at a time with the ↺ and ↻ buttons, or Q and E. Height hides things; turn the field to see behind a wall, or to line up a shot you could not see.
  • Controls: click or tap tiles and buttons, keys 1-9 for menu entries. Right-click or Esc cancels. Drag or pinch to pan and zoom, arrows and +/- as well, 0 to reframe.
